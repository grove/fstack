# Prepare a reliable handoff

Use this recipe when work must continue in a new conversation, worker, or team.
Start with the actual current state and the next task. A handoff should let its
reader resume accurately without assuming access to the previous conversation.

## Establish the state to transfer

Identify the candidate, pending changes, completed work, relevant evidence, and
unresolved questions. Distinguish observations that still apply from checks made
before subsequent changes. Use [verify behavior](../skills/fstack-verify-behavior/SKILL.md)
when a completion claim needs current evidence; a handoff need not pretend all
work is complete before it can be useful.

Use [record decisions](../skills/fstack-record-decisions/SKILL.md) for consequential
choices whose rationale deserves a durable home. The handoff can point to that
record instead of duplicating a long decision history.

## Give the next worker a usable starting point

Use [preserve context](../skills/fstack-preserve-context/SKILL.md) to capture the
goal, boundaries, reliable state, necessary references, and next useful action.
Include access limitations and the scope already authorized. Separate a suggested
next step from an action that has already occurred.

If several workers will proceed, use
[coordinate work](../skills/fstack-coordinate-work/SKILL.md) to define ownership,
dependencies, isolation, and integration responsibilities. A coordination plan
does not by itself dispatch workers or update a tracker.

Use [communicate change](../skills/fstack-communicate-change/SKILL.md) when the
recipient needs a review or decision narrative about the change. That reader may
need purpose and implications more than a detailed continuation note; choose the
form that serves the actual next responsibility.

## Check resumability and stop

Read the handoff as someone without conversation history. Can the reader locate
the relevant state, distinguish supported conclusions from assumptions, and begin
the next authorized task? Remove obsolete detail and repair missing context.

Finish with the handoff or communication draft and its known limitations. Sending
it, assigning work, or publishing a review requires the applicable authorization;
preparing the text alone does not imply those actions occurred.
